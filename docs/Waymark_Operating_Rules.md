# Waymark — Commercial model decisions

Status: pricing **structure** locked 31/08/2026. Price **points** deferred until beta data. Companion to the Commercial model section of Project\_Organization.

---

## 1\. Metering and pricing model — 

**Two axes, two jobs.**

- **Terminals \+ stores** meter the operations side (POS).  
- **Engine depth** meters Almanac — which departments it is switched on for. Never metered by count.

**Pricing model: banded \+ add-on.** Each tier includes a number of terminals. Beyond that, extra terminals are a fixed published price each. The base invoice is predictable; revenue grows with the customer.

**Hard rule — the wall is depth, never data.** Cap what Almanac *does*, not what the store *is*. The POS must always work in full.

---

## 2\. Tier definitions — 

Tiers are defined by **store archetype**, not by feature subtraction.

### Basic — single-till shop (épicerie, boutique, quincaillerie)

**Operations:** everything, uncapped. POS, catalogue, customers, suppliers, purchases, stock, cash, users, exports.

**Almanac:**

- Demand prediction — reorder point and suggested quantity, one product at a time  
- Expiry alerts on a watched-item list (capped count) with suggested markdown timing  
- Stockout/overstock cost — one monthly "what this cost you" figure  
- Seasonality — read-only "your busy days and weeks"

**Design intent:** Basic sees the differentiator (expiry) and sees a money figure. It hits the wall on depth, not on visibility. That is what drives the upgrade.

### Pro — supermarket or multi-department store

**Includes 3 terminals.** Extra terminals at a fixed published add-on price.

Everything in Basic uncapped, plus:

- Expiration-aware **ordering** (the full perishable model, not just alerts)  
- Multi-product ordering optimisation under a budget constraint  
- Substitution effect analysis  
- What-if / price elasticity simulation  
- Full seasonality decomposition with horizons and intervals  
- Supplier lead-time modelling  
- Planning department in full

### Enterprise — multi-store

Everything in Pro per store, plus capabilities different in kind:

- Cross-store comparison and stock transfer suggestions  
- Consolidated purchasing  
- Central catalogue and price governance  
- Role hierarchy (regional vs store manager)  
- Multi-store consent and privacy administration

---

## 3\. Enterprise posture, year one —  (revised 31/08)

**Listed as *sur devis*. Multi-store enquiries are answered with Pro-per-store, not refused.**

The constraint is **never sell capability that does not exist yet** — not "avoid chains".

- Enterprise appears on the pricing page as *sur devis* with a short enquiry form (stores, terminals, categories, current software). No price, no SLA, no delivery date published.  
- When a chain enquires: offer **Pro, one licence per store, available now**. Each store runs standalone at full Pro value. Revenue and a design partner, with nothing promised that is not built.  
- We will **not** sign a contract promising cross-store transfers, consolidated purchasing or central catalogue governance until those ship.  
- We’ll Keep an Enterprise waiting list; those customers get first access and the early-tester terms when multi-store ships.  
- When Enterprise does become real: We’ll quote bespoke (Pro price × stores, with a floor, plus one-off onboarding), SLA written per contract, never published.

**Risk still being managed:** a customer sold multi-store features before support capacity exists churns loudly in a small market.

---

## 4\. Verticals — 

**One product; a store-type question at onboarding switches default modules on or off** (expiry for food, variants for clothing, etc.).

One codebase, one price list, no separate editions. Store type changes *defaults*, never entitlements — entitlements come from the tier alone.

---

# Waymark — Offline behaviour & hosting

Locked 31/08/2026. Companion to System\_Architecture and to   
---

## 1\. Architecture confirmation — hybrid, store-authoritative

The System\_Architecture "Data placement" section already committed to this; it is now explicit.

**Store side (authoritative for all operational data):** Full operational DB — products, prices, inventory, batches, transactions. Direct identifiers (customer name, phone, email, address), staff identifiers, the `customer_id ↔ pseudonym_key` mapping table, consent records and notice versions. Statistics **tier 1**.

**Cloud side:** Statistics **tiers 2–3** (pseudonymised), transaction history keyed by pseudonym, products/batches/suppliers/POs, the Analytical Engine (all five departments), backups.

**Boundary:** Statistics tier 1 → tier 2\. Pseudonymisation happens on the way out of the store. Nothing carrying a direct identifier crosses.  
---

## 2\. Store server hosting —  (tier-shaped)

| Tier | Store database runs on | Hardware required |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Basic (1 terminal) | The terminal itself | None. Nightly cloud backup only |
| Pro (2–3 terminals) | Designated primary terminal | Hot replica on a second terminal **required**; dedicated mini-PC recommended |
| Enterprise | Dedicated store box | Mini-PC required per store |

**Rationale:** requiring hardware at Basic would kill adoption with an épicerie. The hot-replica requirement at Pro removes the single point of failure (a cashier shutting down the primary) without forcing hardware cost on anyone who can't carry it.

This doubles as the first half of the **hardware policy** item.

---

## 3\. Offline has two levels

**Level 1 — store offline from cloud** (internet down). All terminals still reach the local store DB. Every Operations category (A–L) works in full: checkout, refunds against local history, manager-PIN discounts, credit sales against a live local balance. **The only loss is new engine output.** Cached recommendations remain viewable and actionable; accepting one still creates a local Purchase Order.

**Level 2 — terminal offline from the store server** (LAN down, switch dead, primary terminal off). Cannot occur at Basic, where the terminal *is* the server. This is the state where a terminal is genuinely guessing.

**Offline window: unlimited.** A terminal never locks for being offline. Licence enforcement happens at renewal, never at the till.

---

## 4\. Level-2 behaviour — 

Governing pattern: **allow, record, reconcile.** Block only what is physically unresolvable.

| Action | Behaviour at Level 2 |
| :---- | :---- |
| Sale, cash, receipt, price lookup | Full function, always |
| Refund against a past transaction | Allowed if the original is in the terminal's local cache; **blocked** otherwise |
| Cross-store refund (multi-store) | **Blocked** — unresolvable without the cloud. UI must explain, not fail silently |
| Above-threshold discount | The retailer decides whether it’s Allowed or not, if yes; transaction will be **flagged**, surfaced in the Z-report and a "needs review" queue on reconnect |
| Credit sale to a named customer | Allowed against last-known balance, with a per-transaction ceiling while isolated *(ceiling value TBD)* |
| Stock conflicts | Negative stock permitted and reconciled. Never block a sale to protect a number |

**Rationale for flag-don't-block on discounts:** an unverifiable manager override is exactly the hole the Z-report and shift-reconciliation design exists to close. Flagging preserves both the sale and the accountability.

---

## 5\. Engine timing — 

**The engine never participates in a live transaction.**

Almanac pre-computes recommendation candidates keyed by pseudonym and pushes them down to the store. The **store-side half of the Integration Layer** resolves a pseudonym to a real customer at the till, at delivery time only.

Consequences:

- Checkout recommendations keep working offline.  
- The mapping table never leaves the store, satisfying the Integration Layer's own security rule.  
- No latency risk during a sale.  
- Consistent with the brand: an almanac is precomputed by definition.

**Architecture change:** the Integration Layer must be split into a **cloud half** (emits pseudonym-keyed recommendations) and a **store half** (resolves identity, checks `objection_flag`, writes `processing_log`, delivers to POS/Admin).   
---

## 6\. Operator-visible behaviour

- Persistent, calm connectivity indicator — e.g. *"Hors ligne · 14 ventes en attente"*. Never an error dialog, never a modal.  
- **Every Almanac output carries its timestamp and age.** Same principle as the locked voice rule "admit the range" — Almanac admits its age as well as its uncertainty.

---

## 7\. Hosting

**Cloud in Algeria**, as already written into System\_Architecture.

Optionality worth knowing but not spending: because only pseudonymised data crosses the boundary and the mapping table never does, non-Algerian hosting would also be **defensible** under law 25-11 if Algerian providers prove impractical. Law 25-11 imposes no localisation mandate; it requires prior ANPDP authorisation for transfers to non-adequate countries (the US is not on the adequacy list; response 30–60 days).

**Still open:** choice of provider. Also to investigate — any university/data-centre arrangement available through ENSIA.

---

## 8\. Why this architecture is the right one for a global market

Added 31/08 in response to the global-market question

**The local-first hybrid means personal data never leaves the store by construction.** Entering a new jurisdiction therefore means standing up a regional cloud for *pseudonymised aggregates* — not migrating anyone's personal data across a border, and not seeking transfer authorisations for it.

**This is the most global-friendly decision made so far, and it was made for Algerian reasons.** It should not be traded away for convenience later.

It also satisfies four of the five global-readiness rules already in System\_Architecture almost for free: no cross-region data paths, no hardcoded residency assumptions, retention as config, consent text as versioned data.

**Practical consequence:** a new jurisdiction \= a new regional cloud deployment \+ a jurisdiction-specific consent notice version \+ a legal-basis value. Not a re-architecture.

**Open parameters from this document:** Level-2 credit ceiling · Basic backup frequency and retention · recommended mini-PC specification.

# Waymark — Customer lifecycle: founding programme, data rights, lapse

Locked 31/08/2026. Companion to: [Waymark — Commercial model decisions](#waymark-—-commercial-model-decisions)

---

## 1\. The collision, and how it was resolved

"Beta" was doing three jobs at once — a quality expectation, a free period, and a data-rights window — and the three wanted different durations. They are now three separate things.

| Thing | Duration | Starts |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Free period | 3 months | Customer activation |
| Discount period | 12 months | Customer activation |
| Data programme | 12 months | Customer activation |

Discount and data window are deliberately the same term, so the exchange is one sentence in the agreement: **a year at a reduced price, in return for a year of aggregate performance data.**

**Rationale for extending the data window from 3 to 12 months:** three months shows one season, not a cycle. The engine rests on seasonal decomposition, so a full year is the minimum useful window for v2.

---

## 2\. Naming — 

**Customer-facing: "founding customer programme" / *programme des premiers clients*.** **"Beta" is internal vocabulary only and never appears in customer materials.**

Founding-customer framing keeps quality expectations normal and reframes the discount as a reward rather than compensation for defects.  
---

## 3\. Data programme — 

- **Window:** per customer, 12 months from activation. Not calendar-based.  
- **What Waymark receives** is unchanged from the privacy rules: aggregate statistics across at least 20 customers; no individual customer records, pseudonymised or otherwise. No segment stacking, no free-text or outlier values, aggregates held at the store then uploaded.  
- **The agreement must state the data window explicitly and separately from the free period.**

---

## 4\. Renewal — 

**Step-down over a second year** rather than full price at month 13\. The cliff at month 13 is the peak churn moment. Taper values deferred with price points.

### **Free-period expiry**

Distinct from the lapsed-payment ladder in §5, which applies to a customer who has been paying.

Reminders begin before the free period ends and continue at its expiry. The admin app then shows either nothing or a basic reminder to pay. The POS continues for a further 15–30 days with basic operations including data export, so the retailer is aware of the expiry and has time either to pay or to move back to their previous software.

If the retailer does not buy, data collected is no longer used. Only the insights agreed in the founding programme are retained, nothing else.

---

## 5\. Lapsed payment ladder — 

Applies to a **paying** customer who stops paying — distinct from free-period expiry.

| Stage | Day | Behaviour |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Grace** | 0 → 15 | Everything is normal. **15 days, locked** |
| **Degraded** | 16 | **Almanac switches off.** Operations and POS untouched |
| **Suspended** | later | POS drops to sell-and-export only |
| **Retention** | 90 days from suspension | Written notice before deletion |
| **Reactivation** | any time in retention | Everything restored intact |

**Reminder schedule during grace** — enough that no retailer can claim surprise:

| When | Channel |
| :---- | :---- |
| 7 days before due | Admin app \+ SMS/WhatsApp to registered contact |
| Due date | Admin app \+ SMS/WhatsApp |
| Day \+3 | Admin app |
| Day \+7 | Admin app \+ SMS/WhatsApp |
| Day \+11 | Admin app, persistent banner |
| Day \+14 | Final notice: admin app \+ SMS/WhatsApp, states exactly what changes tomorrow and what does not |

**All reminders appear in the admin app only — never at the till, never in front of shoppers.**

**Absolute rule:** *the export button is never disabled, at any stage, ever.* Belongs in the ToS as an explicit promise. It is the direct answer to the migration fear identified as the top adoption risk.

**Legal note:** under 18-07 the retailer is the controller of their shoppers' data and has their own commercial record-retention obligations. Deleting their data could destroy records they are legally required to keep — deletion on non-payment is never immediate and always noticed in writing.  
---

## 6\. Founding programme selection criteria — 

**Selection is portfolio-driven, not first-come-first-served.** The first cohort determines what Almanac learns. Ten clothes shops and the engine never learns perishables.

**Cohort size: 8–12 stores, hard cap 12\.** Beyond that, one person cannot do onboarding, training and support while still building.

### Portfolio requirements — what the engine and the architecture need tested

| Requirement | Minimum | Why |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Perishable-heavy (épicerie, supérette, food) | \~60% of cohort | Expiration-aware ordering is the differentiator and needs real expiry data |
| Non-perishable (clothing, hardware, cosmetics) | 2 | Proves the "one product, module defaults by store type" design |
| Multi-terminal (Pro archetype) | 2 | Pro tier, hot-replica requirement and Level-2 offline are untested otherwise |
| Genuinely poor connectivity | 1 | The entire offline architecture is a hypothesis until a bad line runs on it |
| Migrating from existing software | 1 | Migration is the top adoption risk and has never been tested |

### Per-store eligibility

- **Already tracks stock in some form** — paper, notebook, or old software. A store with no record-keeping gives Almanac nothing to learn from for months and will blame the software.  
- **Has a decision-maker who will use the admin app** — owner or manager, not only cashiers. Recommendations with nobody to accept them produce no feedback loop.  
- **Reachable in person.** Onboarding and training are in-person for this segment.  
- **Named contact reachable by phone/WhatsApp.**  
- **Likely to still be trading in 12 months.** A closure mid-programme costs the whole data window.

### Excluded (from the privacy rules)

- Pharmacies — excluded from v1 entirely.  
- Any category exposing religious practice or comparable sensitive insight.

### What the participant commits to

1. **Runs Waymark as the primary system, not in parallel with the old one.** The single most important commitment — parallel running produces no usable data and no real adoption signal.  
2. Aggregate performance data for 12 months.  
3. A monthly structured check-in, \~20 minutes, phone or in person.  
4. Reports problems rather than silently working around them.  
5. Obtains shopper consent as controller, using the tool provided.

### Admission mechanism

Application **plus a short site visit** — Acceptance is written, so the terms are on record.

**Rejection:** a warm standard decline that offers normal terms later. Same principle as the Enterprise correction.  
---

## 7\. Reference customer / case study clause — 

**Governing principle: permission to use their *data* and permission to use their *name* are separate consents, granted separately.** Bundling them is bad practice and sits badly against 18-07's consent-specificity expectations.

**Three levels, each opt-in independently:**

| Level | What it permits | Default |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **1 — Anonymous metrics** | Published results with no name and no identifying detail | Included in the programme agreement |
| **2 — Named reference** | Naming them privately to prospects; they may take a call from a prospect | Opt-in, revocable |
| **3 — Public case study** | Name, logo, quotes, published | Opt-in per case study, with right of review before publication |

**Terms attaching to all three:**

- **Revocable at any time, with no effect on their service or their price.** A customer must never feel their discount depends on being a marketing asset.  
- **Anonymous is not automatically anonymous at this cohort size.** With \~10 stores, "a supérette in Sétif" identifies someone. Level 1 claims are published only as **aggregates across at least 3 stores**, or with the location generalised to the region. Same instinct as the ≥20-customer aggregation rule.  
- **Any published figure must be one they have seen and confirmed.**  
- **Nothing is published during the free period.** Wait until at least one payment — a testimonial from someone who has not paid is worth little and reads that way.  
- **No shopper-level data appears in a case study, in any form, ever.**  
- Level 3 is revocable with a stated takedown window (30 days).

## 1\. How the ANAE auto-entrepreneur card works

### Legal basis

Law 22-23, with executive decrees 23-196 and 23-197 (25 May 2023\) defining the eligible activity list.

### Is Waymark's activity eligible?

**Yes.** The status covers seven domains, one of which is **digital services** — explicitly including web, mobile and software development.

### Eligibility

- 16+, biometric national ID, resident in Algeria  
- Works **individually** — no permanent employees, no partners or associates  
- Activity on the ANAE list  
- Annual turnover under 5,000,000 DZD  
- No incompatible commercial status, tax compliant

### Getting the card

Entirely online at `anae.dz`. Required: colour copy of biometric ID (both sides), 35×45mm photo on white background, a selfie holding the ID, active email, active Algerian phone number, address.

**No registre de commerce, no capital, no bank guarantee required.**

Processing: **maximum 3 business days.** Approval or rejection is final for that submission — no revision requests; rejection is appealable to the supervising ministry within 30 days. **The card is valid for 5 years.**

### What it costs

## 2\. Decision: Choosing the card — with conversion triggers

**Yes. The auto-entrepreneur card is the right vehicle for year one.**

It is free, approved in three days, permits legitimate invoicing with an NIF, costs about 1% of turnover at scale, and requires no capital, no accountant and no registre de commerce. EURL/SARL cost money, need capital and accounting, and buy nothing Waymark needs yet.

1. **No employees, no partners.** This is the binding constraint, not the money. The moment there is a co-founder or a first hire, the status ends.  
2. **No registre de commerce.** The operative limit for payments — see section 3\.  
3. **5,000,000 DZD ceiling.** At an illustrative 60,000 DZD/store/year that is roughly 83 stores. Not binding in year one or two.

- A customer contract requires a company counterparty.  
- **Selling outside Algeria becomes real** — see section 5\. This is the strongest trigger of the five.

**These triggers tend to fire together**, which is the useful insight: gateway, RC, hiring, export and incorporation are one event.

## 5\. Global market — how it affects the above

### The core point

**Algerian payments and international payments are two entirely separate rails.** CIB/Edahabia/CCP in DZD through SATIM-integrated gateways; international cards and wallets in USD/EUR through global processors. Building one gets none of the other. There is therefore **no efficiency in doing them together and no penalty for doing them years apart.**

So the year-one answer stands: **no gateway needed, and going global later does not change that.**

### What global actually requires, in order

1. **A company that can contract abroad.** An auto-entrepreneur realistically cannot — no RC, no employees, and foreign B2B buyers expect a company counterparty.  
2. **A lawful way to receive and repatriate foreign currency.** Bank of Algeria **Regulation 26-02 (23 July 2026\)** requires repatriation of non-hydrocarbon export proceeds — including services — **within 120 days** of service completion, extendable to 180 with export credit insurance, with payment terms documented in the contract. **\[To be VERIFIED\]** whether and how this applies to an individual auto-entrepreneur exporting digital services.  
3. **Per-jurisdiction data deployment** — already a stated architecture principle.  
4. **A localised product** — Arabic/RTL is needed for Algeria anyway; French and English follow.  
5. **Local support** in local language and hours.  
6. **An international payment method.**

**Payment is sixth, not first.** Items 1 and 2 are the real gates, and both are company-level problems.

### The realistic international path

**Merchant of record**, not a payment gateway. A MoR (Lemon Squeezy, Paddle) becomes the legal seller, handles VAT/sales tax in every jurisdiction, and pays out to you.

- **Stripe does not support Algeria** as a business location. Confirmed against Stripe's own country list.  
- **Lemon Squeezy currently lists Algeria for bank payouts** — a viable route without incorporating abroad.  
- **\[To be VERIFIED\] risk:** Lemon Squeezy is being folded into Stripe Managed Payments per their 2026 update, and Stripe does not support Algeria. Country support could change. Re-check before relying on it.

Incorporating abroad (Estonia e-Residency, UAE) is the other common MENA route, but it is a serious step with real compliance cost and should not be considered before there is export revenue to justify it.

### What global changes in already-locked decisions

| Decision | Effect |
| :---- | :---- |
| Tier structure (terminals \+ engine depth) | **None.** Currency- and country-agnostic. Store archetypes translate directly |
| Banded \+ add-on pricing model | **None.** Price *points* become per-market — price in local currency, never convert from DZD |
| Founding customer programme | **None.** Algeria-only, year one |
| Lapse ladder / 90-day retention | Retention must be **config, not a constant** — already an architecture rule |
| Offline \+ hosting architecture | **Strongly positive.** See below |
| Legal vehicle | Adds the strongest conversion trigger |
| Brand (Waymark / Almanac) | **None.** Both travel |

### Why the architecture already chosen is the right one for global

The local-first hybrid means **personal data never leaves the store by construction**. Entering a new jurisdiction therefore means standing up a regional cloud for *pseudonymised aggregates* — not migrating anyone's personal data across a border. That is a far smaller and far safer operation than a cloud-first design would face.  
This is the single most global-friendly decision made so far, and it was made for Algerian reasons.

### Note on global-readiness

System Architecture lists six cheap global-readiness rules, **Everything beyond those six rules is premature.** We won’t build multi-currency billing, tax engines or MoR integration now. Global-readiness that costs real time before there is a single foreign customer is a mistake to a solo founder.

# Waymark — Privacy rules

Moved from Project\_Organization 01/09/2026. Governed by laws 18-07 and 25-11. Assessed in Waymark\_DPIA\_v1. Actions to complete the agreement are scheduled for October 2026\.

**1\. Nothing is decided automatically.** The software produces recommendations the retailer is free to accept or reject.

**2\. Customer-affecting matters are informational only.** On anything touching a customer's terms — refusing credit, tier changes — the engine may suggest or inform. It may never decide.

**3\. Pseudonymisation is the boundary.** Private data, customer data above all, is handled through the pseudonymisation process the law defines. The Statistics module is responsible for preventing such data crossing to the engine side.

**4\. Sensitive categories are excluded.** The system does not process categories that expose private insight into shoppers. v1 is not to be used with pharmacies, nor with any category revealing religious practice.

**5\. Shoppers may refuse, at two levels.** They may decline to have an account at all and be recorded as *other* transactions; or they may keep an account but refuse personalised suggestions, and so not be treated explicitly by the engine.

**6\. Waymark receives only aggregates.** Aggregate statistics computed across at least 20 customers. No individual customer records, pseudonymised or otherwise, are transmitted for development. No segment stacking, no free-text or outlier values, aggregates held at the store then uploaded.

**7\. The retailer is the controller.** They obtain shopper consent and record the response in the app, and inform shoppers that data is processed for their own benefit. Engine features are off by default; the retailer configures what to use; shopper rights are handled through a tool Waymark provides.

**Data Processing Agreement.** Waymark processes the retailer's customers' data on their behalf. The DPA defines that relationship and remains subject to the law.

# Waymark — Liability limitation & data ownership/portability

Locked 31/08/2026. Item 5 of the commercial/legal list.

> **This is a decision list, not a contract.** It specifies what the ToS/CGU clauses must say. The clauses themselves need a lawyer before signature. Nothing here is legal advice.

## Part A — Liability limitation

### A1. The nature of what Waymark produces

Stated plainly in the contract, because it is the foundation of everything else:

- **Almanac produces recommendations, forecasts and estimates.** Not instructions, not decisions.  
- **No output executes automatically.** Every recommendation is accepted, adjusted or dismissed by the retailer. Accepting a reorder recommendation is what creates a Purchase Order; nothing creates one on its own.  
- **Forecasts are probabilistic.** Every figure is presented with its interval. **A forecast that turns out wrong is not a defect** — it is a forecast. This must be said explicitly, or "the software told me to order 240 and I was left with stock" becomes an argument.

The prediction interval shown on every engine number is therefore not only a design decision — it is the evidence that uncertainty was disclosed at the moment of the decision.

### A2. Decisions remain the retailer's

Commercial responsibility for the following stays entirely with the retailer:

- What to order, how much, and when  
- Pricing, promotions and markdown decisions  
- Credit decisions and customer terms  
- Supplier selection  
- Stock counts and physical inventory accuracy

Consistent with the privacy position already recorded: the engine may inform or suggest on matters affecting customers (credit, tiers), but never decides them.

### A3. What Waymark *is* responsible for

A clause that disclaims everything reads as evasive and may not be enforceable. Waymark accepts responsibility for:

- The software performing substantially as described  
- **Data integrity and safety** — no loss or corruption of the retailer's records through Waymark's fault  
- Backups performed according to the stated policy  
- Security of the cloud side and of data in transit  
- **Honouring the export promise at all times** (Part B)  
- Its obligations as data processor for the retailer's shopper data

### A4. What is excluded

- Indirect, incidental and consequential loss  
- Lost profits, lost revenue, lost goodwill, lost business opportunity  
- Losses arising from the retailer's own data entry errors, stock-count inaccuracy, or failure to act on information available to them  
- Losses arising from the retailer's hardware, power supply or internet connection — **noting that the POS is designed to keep working offline indefinitely**, so a connectivity failure should not cause loss  
- Third-party services outside Waymark's control

### A5. The cap — 

**Liability is capped at the total fees paid by that retailer in the 12 months preceding the claim.**

**Carve-outs that are not capped** (and in many regimes cannot be):

- Gross negligence and wilful misconduct  
- Personal-data breaches attributable to Waymark  
- Death or personal injury

**\[To be VERIFIED\]** perhaps with a lawyer which carve-outs Algerian law makes mandatory, and whether a cap expressed as fees-paid is enforceable as drafted.

### A6. The retailer's own obligations

Stated because they bound Waymark's exposure:

- Accurate entry of products, prices, costs, expiry dates and deliveries  
- Periodic physical stock counts  
- **Acting as data controller** for their shoppers: obtaining consent, using the provided tool, honouring shopper rights  
- Keeping their own commercial and fiscal records as the law requires of them  
- Reporting faults rather than working around them

---

## Part B — Data ownership & portability

### B1. The retailer owns their data — 

Explicitly and without qualification, the retailer owns:

- Their catalogue, prices and cost data  
- All transactions and sales history  
- Customer records and loyalty data  
- Supplier records and purchase orders  
- Stock, batch and movement records  
- Staff records  
- The record of recommendations issued to them and the decisions they took on each

That last item matters: the recommendation/decision log is **their business record**, not Waymark's, and it must be exportable. It is also what lets them prove a decision was theirs.

### B2. What Waymark owns — 

- The software, the models, the algorithms and the engine itself  
- **The aggregate statistics collected under the founding customer programme** — computed across at least 20 customers, non-identifying, per the privacy rules

Ownership of the aggregates is stated explicitly rather than left implied.

The programme agreement must make the exchange visible.

### B3. Export — 

| Property | Commitment |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Availability** | At any time, self-service, no request needed |
| **Format** | Open and documented — CSV and/or JSON. Never a proprietary format |
| **Scope** | Complete, including full history. Not a summary, not a sample |
| **Cost** | Free. No fee, ever |
| **Delay** | None imposed. No queue, no waiting period |

### B4. The absolute promise — 

> **The export function is never disabled. Not during grace, not when degraded, not when suspended, not at any point before deletion.**

This belongs in the ToS as an explicit commitment, not buried in a policy. It is the direct answer to the migration fear identified as the top adoption risk, and no incumbent in this market matches it.

### B5. On termination

1. Export remains available throughout the **90-day retention window** after suspension.  
2. **Written notice before deletion.**  
3. Deletion after the retention window, unless the retailer has asked for an extension.  
4. Reactivation within the window restores everything intact.

**Legal note:** under 18-07 the retailer is the controller of their shoppers' data. Waymark deleting their data could destroy records they are required to keep. Deletion is never automatic-and-silent, and the notice must tell them to export first.

### B6. No lock-in — stated as commitments

- No proprietary export format  
- No export fee  
- Migration *in* assistance is offered (per the onboarding policy); migration *out* is never obstructed

### B7. Deletion on request

The retailer may request deletion of their data at any time. Waymark will confirm in writing and execute it — subject to the retailer's own legal retention obligations.

## 2\. Messaging hierarchy — 

Three claims, ranked. Ranking is **value → trust → risk removal**, each answering a real fear in the order a retailer has them.

### Claim 1 — Stop paying for spoilage and stockouts

*Waymark forecasts demand product by product and tells you what to order and when — taking expiry dates into account.*

**Answers:** "what do I actually get for my money?"

Proof points:

- Expiration-aware ordering — the locally distinctive capability  
- A monthly figure showing what stockouts and spoilage actually cost  
- Reorder points and quantities computed from your own store's history, not a rule of thumb

**Lead with this.** It is the only claim that is about money, and it is the reason anyone pays.

### Claim 2 — Every suggestion explains itself, and you decide

*Each recommendation shows the reasoning behind it and the range around it. Accept it, adjust it, or ignore it. Nothing happens on its own.*

**Answers:** "Am I losing control of my shop?"

Proof points:

- The Because block — at most three reasons, always with figures  
- Every number carries its range  
- Accept / Adjust / Dismiss on every recommendation — the middle option is the point  
- Engine features are off by default; you switch on what you want

### Claim 3 — It keeps working when the internet doesn't, and your data is always yours

*The till never stops for a connection problem. And you can export everything, at any time, free — including if you leave.*

**Answers:** "what happens when it fails, and am I trapped?"

Proof points:

- Unlimited offline operation; the terminal never locks  
- Export never disabled, at any stage  
- Open formats, complete history, no fee

## 3\. What NOT to lead with

A discipline worth writing down, because the temptation is real and the machinery underneath is genuinely sophisticated.

**Do not lead with:** artificial intelligence · analytics · optimisation · operations research · ARIMA, CP-SAT or any method name · "smart" anything · the number of features.

Two reasons:

1. **The buyer does not care.** A shopkeeper buys less spoilage, not a solver.  
2. **"AI" raises exactly the fear the product is built to answer.** Every autonomy signal in the marketing has to be undone in the sales conversation, and it sits badly against the legal position that nothing is decided automatically.

The sophistication is a reason the claims are *true*. It is not the claim.
