Brand book

The usage rules. Every sentence names a token.

The store tells you what it needs. You decide what happens.

Waymark is the product an operator uses. Almanac is the forecasting and ordering engine inside it. One identity, two roles: Waymark owns the mark, the violet and every button the operator presses; Almanac owns the cyan, the forecasts, and nothing the user clicks. Almanac is never sold separately, never given an icon, and never introduces a second palette or a second voice.

Five traits decide every call below: trust, sophistication, simplicity, smart, insight-based. The engine watches sales velocity, shelf state and supplier lead times, then tells you what it would do. Every recommendation is a suggestion you can accept, adjust or ignore.

The seven locked lines

Hold these; everything else is negotiable. Anything beyond them is a second brand to maintain.

  1. The name. Waymark Almanac, short form Almanac.
  2. The scope. Almanac is the whole analytical layer — forecasting, optimisation, elasticity, substitution — not the forecaster alone.
  3. The descriptor. Forecasting & ordering engine, on first appearance in every document.
  4. The register split. Archivo is Waymark, IBM Plex Mono is Almanac.
  5. The visibility line. In-app, Almanac appears on insight surfaces and consent screens only.
  6. Two semantic colours. Critical and warning. No positive — violet already confirms.
  7. Label before colour. A card with an edge and no label is not a valid state.

Voice

Write forecasts in the future with a figure, observations in the present, incidents in the past. Say "will run out in 3 days," never "is low." A forecast without a number cannot be judged, so never ship one.

Suggest, never instruct. Write "Suggested reorder: 240 units," not "Reorder 240 units." The imperative belongs to the operator; the engine's job is to make a case.

Admit the range. State no number without its interval, and say when confidence is low. An engine that is never uncertain is never believed twice.

Give every recommendation a Because block — three reasons or fewer, each naming a figure. A recommendation the operator cannot interrogate is an instruction, and instructions break the promise the brand is built on.

Keep the quiet state. An engine that only speaks when something is wrong trains people to dread it, so ship the inline, action-free quiet weight and let it be ignorable forever.

Almanac is always a name, never a verb. Never write "Almanac'd," "almanacking," or "the Almanac says" — write "Almanac · Forecast" as a label and let the sentence speak plainly. Set the product name as Waymark, the engine as ALMANAC in caps wherever it is a label, and the descriptor as Forecasting & ordering engine in sentence case.

Colour

Colour never carries a state alone. Every weight is identified by its label first; the rule reinforces the label and never replaces it. The diamond marker is the second channel — shape is what colour cannot take away.

Ship two semantic colours, not three. The best yellow-green available sits ΔE2000 = 25 from cyan.signal and a normal green reaches 21 — inside the range where two colours read as one family. Red and green collapse to ΔE 6 under deuteranopia, and around 8% of men have red-green colour vision deficiency; on a shop floor that is not an edge case. Violet already owns every confirmation, and here the positive state is the absence of a warning, which needs no colour at all.

Build surfaces from surface-page and surface-card: the page is the ground, the card is what sits on it, and they never swap. Use surface-tile for quiet non-card fills — avatars, badges, selected rows. Never fill a card with cyan.tint; a tint is for interval bars and quiet fills, and an Almanac card stays surface-card with a cyan.signal edge in both themes.

Set body copy in text, supporting sentences in text-secondary, and timestamps, SKUs and column labels in text-muted. In dark, keep text-muted on surface-page — inside a card it falls to 4.44:1, so use text-secondary there.

Label every action fill with on-action, not literal white. action lightens to accent-violet in dark, where white reaches only 4.05:1 and ink-black reaches 4.61:1.

Draw every focus ring in focus-ring, border-nav wide, offset 2px. It holds above 3:1 on every surface in both themes.

States

Move a filled control's ground away from its label on interaction, never toward it: action-hover darkens in light and lightens in dark, and action-pressed goes one step further in the same direction. on-action is then the label in every state, so it never flips colour mid-interaction. Hover a secondary control by moving its border and label to action; hover a ghost control by moving its label to text.

Render a disabled control on border with a text-secondary label — 7.77:1 in light, 6.97:1 in dark. Never disable by lowering opacity; that drags the label under the floor and makes the control look broken rather than unavailable.

Contrast floors

Hold text to 4.5:1 on the ground its token's usage note names — surface-card and surface-page, or the semantic tint where the card is tinted — and 3:1 at 24px or bold 19px and above. Hold rules, edges, borders that carry meaning, icons and focus rings to 3:1. Every text token in this system clears 4.5:1 on both grounds in both themes, with no exceptions.

The signal colours are measured as marks, not as text, which is why warning.signal at 3.17:1 and cyan.signal at 4.11:1 are correct and in the ramp: they are 3px rules, edges and fills, and never text. border and border-subtle are decorative separators with no floor at all — meaning is carried by the label and the signal rule, so a reader who cannot see the border loses nothing.

Typography

Set everything readable in Archivo and every label, SKU and quantity in IBM Plex Mono. The split is load-bearing, not decorative: Archivo is Waymark, Plex Mono is Almanac, and a reader tells a measurement from a claim by the face alone.

Set a figure inside a sentence in data-inline, so "runs out in 3 days" changes face mid-line. Set stat-tile figures in data-lg, table figures in data, and interval endpoints in data-sm.

Set every attribution label in label, caps, and never in Archivo and never in violet. Reserve engine-name for the lockup alone. Use display only on a deck cover or a marketing hero — never in the product.

Set a mono figure at the same size as the Archivo around it. The two faces are metric-matched to within 2% — Archivo's x-height is 0.526em against Plex Mono's 0.516em, and their cap heights are 0.686em and 0.698em — so no optical size correction is needed, and any you add will read as a mistake.

Both faces ship here as Latin-subset woff2 under fonts/, and both are free on Google Fonts, so the identity carries across app, decks and print with no licensing work.

Space, borders and radii

Work on a 4px grid. Pad a row-scale card space-4 and a full-width recommendation space-6; leave space-2 between adjacent buttons and space-6 between cards.

Round buttons radius-md, cards and stat tiles radius-lg, inputs and the interval bar radius-sm, avatars and the mark's point radius-full.

Give controls a control-md minimum height on desktop and control-lg on anything touched. Waymark runs on a shop floor as well as a desk: 40px is comfortable under a pointer but misses the 44px target floor under a thumb, so a primary action on a touch surface takes control-lg. Reserve control-sm for dense table rows under a pointer.

Draw borders border-hairline in border, and row dividers inside a card in border-subtle. Reserve border-signal for the one rule that names a weight: top edge for forecast, warning and critical; left edge for quiet. Nothing else in the system is 3px.

Never use a shadow to lift a card. Surfaces separate by ground and border — surface-card on surface-page, border around it. There is no shadow family here because the brand does not use one.

The mark

A chevron moving toward a fixed point: the system suggests a direction, and the marker is the decision that stays with the operator. It is built from squares, circles and diamonds so it survives a 16px favicon and a printed sign alike.

Keep clear space equal to the diameter of the point on all four sides, and double it on signage. Nothing crosses it. The delivered SVGs carry 7 of those 12 units at the sides and 8 above and below, so add the remainder in layout — or use an icon tile, which already carries the full measure.

Hold 24px as the app minimum and 16px as the favicon floor. Below 16px, drop to the mark alone. Below 14px type, use the icon tile instead of the lockup. The print floor is 8mm mark height.

Take every app icon, tab icon and favicon from the Icons group — never a bare mark on a square you cut yourself. Ship waymark-favicon.svg as the browser favicon: it carries the violet colourway on light chrome and the dark-UI colourway on dark, with no theme switching in markup. Use the rounded tile where the platform applies no mask and the full-bleed square where it does, because iOS, Android and the stores round the corners themselves. Almanac never gets a tile of its own.

Never rotate it — the mark points right. Never stretch or condense it. Never colour it outside the brand ramp, and never set a violet mark on violet: go to waymark-mark-reverse.svg. Cyan is not an alternative mark colour either; cyan belongs to the engine and never touches the chevron.

Almanac in product

Almanac has three in-product forms and no others, all legal only on insight surfaces and consent screens, inside a product already showing the Waymark mark:

  • Panel header — ALMANAC in label-lg over a border-hairline cyan.signal rule the width of the word.
  • Attribution label — ALMANAC · FORECAST in label, no rule. This appears hundreds of times a day.
  • Diamond marker — an 8–16px square turned 45° in cyan.signal, always adjacent to the label.

Never give Almanac a logo, monogram or letter-A tile. Never let the diamond stand alone or exceed 16px. Never pair the mark with Almanac without the Waymark word. Never put Almanac on a login screen, invoice or receipt — that relationship is with Waymark.

Iconography

The system ships no icon set. The only drawn marks are the Waymark chevron-and-point under assets/Marks/ and the Almanac diamond, which is a text-level marker, not an icon. Choose an outline set at 1.5px stroke on a 24px grid and colour it text-muted at rest and action when active; state that choice here once it is made rather than mixing sets. Every SVG under assets/ is served as an <img> and cannot inherit colour, so each file carries its own ink — pick the colourway, do not recolour it in CSS.

App icons

App icons, tab icons and favicons live in the Icons group, not here. Do not cut your own square from a bare mark: the padding and the corner will not match.

Artboard

The mark files are square, with the artwork centred: 7 units clear left and right, 8 above and below, on the 64-unit grid. Clear space is one point diameter — 12 units — so a bare mark still needs about 5 more units of space at the sides in layout. The icon tiles already carry the full measure.

The deck's own artwork was not centred: it sat 10 units from the left and 4 from the right, which threw the mark off-centre in any square box and broke its own clear-space rule on the right. The geometry is unchanged; only the artboard window moved.

Geometry

On the 64-unit construction grid: chevron 23,8 47,32 23,56 10,56 34,32 10,8 — a 45° stroke 13 units wide measured horizontally, spanning y 8 to 56 with its tip at x 47; point r 6 centred at 54,32. The viewBox is offset +3 in x to centre it. The 7-unit gap between chevron tip and point centre is fixed — it is the pause before a decision and must never close.

Minimum sizes: 24px in app, 16px favicon, 8mm printed. Below 16px use the mark alone; below a 20px wordmark drop the Almanac lockup and set Waymark alone.

Provenance

The SVGs were rebuilt from the brand deck's raster artwork on the construction grid and render pixel-identical to it (max channel difference 19/255, on antialiased edges only). They are a reconstruction, not the original vector source — if an .ai or .svg master exists, replace these files with it.

Every PNG here and in Icons is rendered from those SVGs at its target size. The deck's own rasters are not shipped: it held one usable copy at 840px and four small slide placements at 300, 212, 160 and 68px, which are below every floor this system sets. The deck remains the provenance record; these files are the artwork.

Note: the deck's construction slide reads "spans 8 to 47 on the x-axis"; the artwork itself spans x 10 to 47. These files follow the artwork.

The primary Waymark · Almanac lockup, for decks, pricing, docs and contracts. A standalone Almanac setting reads as a company name however it is drawn; a lockup that structurally requires the parent word cannot.

What the consumer provides

One size, --w, the Waymark wordmark's font size. Every other measure is a multiple of it, so the lockup scales without being redrawn:

Part Spec
Mark waymark-mark-indigo.svg at 1.1 × --w, then 0.55 × --w of space
Wordmark Archivo 600 at --w, -0.015em, in wordmark — ink-indigo on light grounds, white on dark
Divider border-hairline in cyan.signal at the wordmark's cap height, 0.2 × --w before it and 0.37 × --w after. Never a slash, dot or bullet.
Engine name engine-name at 0.55 × --w, caps, tracked 0.16em, in almanac-text
Descriptor FORECASTING & ORDERING ENGINE in mono at 0.32 × --w, in text-muted, aligned to the mark's left edge, 0.3 × --w below

Alignment

Set the mark, the wordmark, the divider and the engine name on one baseline, in one line box — never by centring the boxes against each other, which floats the engine name and the divider above the wordmark because their cap heights differ.

The divider's foot sits on the baseline and its head at the wordmark's cap height, 0.72 × --w. The mark is centred on that cap band, which puts 0.19 × --w of it below the baseline and 0.91 × --w above — the same proportion the deck draws.

Set the descriptor on first appearance in every document. The name alone reads soft; the descriptor buys back the rigour.

Minimum

The lockup dies below a 20px wordmark. Drop the descriptor first, then drop to Waymark alone — never set Almanac illegibly. On a dark ground, swap the mark to waymark-mark-dark-ui.svg; wordmark and almanac-text flip on their own, and the divider stays cyan.signal in both themes. On royal violet or a photograph, use waymark-mark-reverse.svg with a white wordmark.

Don't

  • Never set the engine name in Archivo, and never in violet.
  • Never use the mark plus Almanac without the Waymark word.
  • Never put the lockup on a login screen, invoice or receipt. That relationship is with Waymark alone.
  • Never replace the divider with a slash, dot, bullet or dash.

The clear space and minimum-size rules for the mark, as one board.

Clear space equals x, the diameter of the mark's point — 12 units on the 64-unit construction grid, or 3/16 of the mark's full width. Reserve it on all four sides. Nothing crosses it: no type, no rule, no photograph edge, no other logo. On signage, double it.

What the consumer provides

The container. Measure x from the mark's own bounding box, not from the artboard the SVG ships on.

The delivered mark files already carry 7 of the 12 units at the sides and 8 above and below, so a bare mark needs roughly 5 more units of space in layout — about 8% of its height. An icon tile carries the full measure already; use one rather than padding a mark by hand.

Below 16px, drop to the mark alone. Below 14px type, use the icon tile rather than the lockup. The mark is built from squares, circles and diamonds so it holds from a 76px app tile down to a 16px favicon without being redrawn — never draw a simplified small-size variant.

Don't

  • Never let a caption, badge or crop edge enter the clear space.
  • Never measure clear space from the point's centre. It is the full diameter.
  • Never scale below the floors to make a layout fit; change the layout.

The four ways the mark will get broken, shown as a board. Use it in onboarding and in any handoff to an agency or a print supplier.

The four

  1. Rotation. The mark points right. The chevron moving toward the point is the whole idea; tilting it makes it decoration. Never rotate, mirror or flip it.
  2. Off-ramp colour. No colours outside the brand ramp. Use one of the five files under assets/Marks/; never tint, gradient or duotone the mark, and never let a photo show through it.
  3. Distortion. Never stretch or condense. Scale uniformly, always — set one dimension and let the other follow.
  4. Violet on violet. A royal-violet or ink-indigo mark on a violet ground loses the chevron. Go to waymark-mark-reverse.svg.

Cyan is not an alternative mark colour either. The mark is Waymark's; cyan.signal belongs to the engine and never touches the chevron.

What the consumer provides

Nothing — this is a reference board. The original deck artwork for the first three cases is in assets/Diagrams/.

Also never

  • Never close the 7-unit gap between chevron tip and point. It is the pause before a decision.
  • Never redraw the mark at small sizes. It holds to 16px as built.
  • Never add a wordmark inside the SVG. The wordmark is type, set live — see Lockup.

The three forms Almanac takes inside the product, and the only three it may take. All are legal on insight surfaces and consent screens only, inside a product already showing the Waymark mark.

The three forms

Form Type Rule Where
Panel header label-lg, caps border-hairline in cyan.signal, the width of the word Once above an Almanac panel
Attribution label label, caps, in almanac-text none On every card, row or figure the engine produced
Diamond marker an 8–16px square turned 45° in cyan.signal — Adjacent to the label, leading it

What the consumer provides

The text after the middot: FORECAST, REORDER, WARNING, CRITICAL, or nothing. Set the word ALMANAC first, then ·, then the qualifier. The label is what identifies a state — InsightCard colours its rule to match, but the label is never optional and never replaced by the colour.

Use the diamond where a row has no other Almanac signal — a table cell, a list item, a toast. It is a text-level marker, sized to the label's cap height, not an icon.

Don't

  • Never set Almanac in Archivo, and never in violet.
  • Never give Almanac a logo, monogram or letter-A tile.
  • Never let the diamond stand alone, exceed 16px, or sit apart from its label.
  • Never use cyan.signal for the label text — that is almanac-text, which is cyan.text in light and cyan.dark in dark. cyan.signal is 4.11:1 on white and is an edge colour, not a text colour.
  • Never put Almanac on a login screen, invoice or receipt.
  • Never write Almanac as a verb. It is always a name.

InsightCard

Almanac

One card, one variable: the colour and position of the rule

Almanac

Milk is running 18% above last Thursday.

Quiet. Inline, no actions. Safe to ignore forever.

Almanac · Forecast

Pain de mie runs out in 2 days.

Standard. A forecast, a suggested response, a way to change it.

Almanac · Warning

Supplier lead time moved from 4 to 6 days.

Warning. Something changed that costs money if ignored. No deadline.

Almanac · Critical

Cold chain at Store 04 broke for 40 min.

Critical. A deadline. If everything is critical, nothing is.

One white card with one variable: the colour and position of the border-signal rule. The label moves with it. There is no positive weight — a shelf that is fine gets no card at all.

The four weights

Weight Rule Label Actions
quiet left, cyan.signal ALMANAC in almanac-text none
forecast top, cyan.signal ALMANAC · FORECAST in almanac-text primary + adjust
warning top, warning.signal ALMANAC · WARNING in warning-text one review action
critical top, critical.signal ALMANAC · CRITICAL in critical-text one incident action

Use quiet for an observation that is safe to ignore forever — it is the weight that keeps the engine from being dreaded. Use warning when something changed that costs money if ignored, with no deadline. Reserve critical for a deadline: if everything is critical, nothing is.

What the consumer provides

The weight, the label qualifier, one claim sentence, and the actions. The card supplies the ground, the border, the rule and the type.

Set the claim in body for quiet and body-strong for the other three — one sentence, one card. Set every figure inside it in data-inline, so the face changes mid-line and a reader tells a measurement from a claim. Write forecasts in the future with a number: "runs out in 2 days," never "is low."

Pad space-4 at row scale. The card ground is surface-card in both themes — an Almanac card is never filled with cyan.tint.

Don't

  • Never ship a card with an edge and no label. That is not a valid state.
  • Never use colour as the only channel. The label names the weight; the rule reinforces it.
  • Never add a positive or success weight. Violet already owns confirmation, and a shelf that is fine gets no card.
  • Never give quiet an action. If it needs one, it is a forecast.
  • Never set the claim in cyan.signal, and never colour a whole card by weight — only the rule and the label change.
  • Never stack more than one primary action. The middle option — Adjust — is what makes the card a suggestion.

RecommendationCard

Almanac

The number, its range, and the Because block

Almanac · Reorder 14:02

Suggested reorder: 240 units of Lait Candia 1L

205  240  280
Because
  • Velocity is 42/day, up from 31/day.
  • Stock covers 3 days; lead time is 4 days.
  • Same week last year ran 1.6× normal.

Why the range

The engine never states a number without its interval. That does more for “suggestion, not instruction” than any wording could.

Why Because

A recommendation the operator cannot interrogate is an instruction, and instructions break the promise. Three reasons or fewer, always mono where a figure appears.

The full-width form of an Almanac recommendation: a suggested number, its interval, the reasons behind it, and three actions. This is the identity in practice — a recommendation the operator cannot interrogate is an instruction, and instructions break the promise the brand rests on.

Anatomy

Part Spec
Rule border-signal in cyan.signal, top edge
Label diamond + ALMANAC · REORDER in label, almanac-text; timestamp in data-sm, text-muted, right
Claim heading-sm, figures in mono at the same size
Interval an 8px interval-track, radius-sm, with 2px cyan.signal caps at both ends and a border-signal stem at the suggested value's true position inside the range; endpoints in data-sm, the value in almanac-text
Because up to three reasons in body-sm, text-secondary, each naming a figure in mono
Actions primary, secondary, ghost — in that order

Pad space-6. Separate Because and the action row from the body with border-subtle, never with a shadow.

What the consumer provides

The suggested quantity, the interval bounds, the product, the reasons, and what the three actions do. Everything else is fixed.

The three actions

Ship all three. Review order is the primary; Adjust quantity is the middle option the brand rests on; Dismiss is the ghost. Two actions remove the middle option and turn a suggestion into a yes/no instruction.

Rules

  • State no number without its range, and put the number on the bar. A bar that shows only the endpoints tells the operator the spread but not where the suggestion sits inside it — which is the one thing the card is about. A suggestion at 240 in a 205–280 range is not the same recommendation as 240 in a 235–245 range, and the stem's position is what shows that at a glance.
  • If the interval is wide, say confidence is low — an engine that is never uncertain is never believed twice.
  • Three reasons or fewer. Each one names a figure, and every figure is mono.
  • Lead with the future: "Suggested reorder: 240 units," not "sold 240 last week." Almanac reads backward-looking if you let it; fix that in language, not in the name.
  • The card ground is surface-card in both themes. Cyan appears only as the top edge, the label, the interval track and its caps.

Don't

  • Never fill the card or its ground with cyan.signal or cyan.tint. A violet or cyan fill makes the engine look like it is acting rather than advising.
  • Never use action for the label or the figures — violet belongs to the operator.
  • Never invert the palette by making the primary button white on a coloured ground; the primary button is the most branded thing on the card.
  • Never drop the Because block to save space. Drop a reason instead.

Variants

States

Three actions, not two. Review is the primary, Adjust is the middle option the brand rests on, Dismiss is the ghost. Removing Adjust turns a suggestion into a yes/no instruction.

Hover darkens in light and lightens in dark, so the ground always moves away from the label and on-action never changes mid-interaction. Use control-lg on touch surfaces — 40px is comfortable with a pointer but misses the 44px target floor on a shop floor.

The operator's control. Violet belongs to the person, so every button, link and active state is action and nothing the engine produces ever looks clickable.

Variants

Variant Ground Label Use

Ship one primary per card. Ship all three on a recommendation: removing Adjust turns a suggestion into a yes/no instruction.

Sizes

Size Height Type Padding inline

default control-md (40) body-sm space-4

Set the height from the token and centre the label; do not let padding decide it, or two buttons with different labels end up different heights.

Use lg on anything touched. Waymark runs on a shop floor: 40px is comfortable under a pointer but misses the 44px target floor under a thumb. Reserve sm for dense table rows under a pointer.

States

Hover moves the ground away from the label — action-hover is darker in light and lighter in dark — and pressed goes one step further to action-pressed. The label stays on-action throughout, so it never flips colour mid-interaction. White holds 9.66:1 on hover and 12.29:1 on pressed; ink-black holds 6.12:1 and 10.08:1.

Hover on secondary moves the border and label to action; hover on ghost moves the label to text.

Disabled renders on border with a text-secondary label — 7.77:1 in light, 6.97:1 in dark — and cursor: not-allowed. Never disable by lowering opacity: it drags the label under the floor and reads as broken rather than unavailable.

Draw focus as a border-nav focus-ring outline at 2px offset. It clears 3:1 on every surface in both themes, so it is the same ring everywhere — do not tint it per variant.

What the consumer provides

The label and the handler. Write labels as verbs the operator performs — "Review order", "Adjust quantity", "Open incident" — never as instructions the engine issues.

Don't

  • Never colour a button cyan.signal, cyan.text or any Almanac colour. Nothing the engine owns is clickable.
  • Never put white on action in dark. Use on-action; white on accent-violet is 4.05:1 and misses AA.
  • Never use a critical.signal or warning.signal fill as a button. Those are edges and fills on cards, not controls.
  • Never invert a card so the primary button becomes the least branded thing on screen.
